October 2015

An old question, built again.

Håkon grew up in Norway with Thor Heyerdahl's Kon-Tiki expedition in the national imagination. A century after Heyerdahl's birth, the Kon-Tiki2 team set out to test what a balsawood raft could still teach us.

“Being Norwegian, I grew up with tales of Thor Heyerdahl's brave Kon-Tiki expedition.”

Callao, Peru

Rope, timber, and trust.

The volunteers tied natural fibre around the logs. Echo sounders went underneath. Ancient construction and modern instruments occupied the same raft.

“No nails or screws!”

30 October

Flags. A blessing. Open water.

The Peruvian Navy welcomed the expedition into its shipyard and marked the launch with ceremony. A few final days of electronics work followed. Then the two rafts left Callao.

Launch ceremony image from the Kon-Tiki2 expedition archive, 2015.

Callao Rapa Nui

7 November

The ocean became the interface.

Seven people on each raft learned to steer by raising and lowering guara boards. Feedback arrived immediately: a changed heading, a wet deck, another wave through the cabin.

“We quickly learnt how to adjust guara boards up or down to set a course.”

Science at sea

An ancient form carried modern questions.

Surface
Manta trawls searched for plastics.
Current
Drifters mapped the water's movement.
Depth
A CTD descended 2,046 metres and came back.

Daily life

Oatmeal. Flying fish. Gale-force wind.

Håkon's account stays wonderfully practical. The crew ate, watched wildlife, worked, rested, and documented Pacific sunsets.

The electrons

Someone had to keep the stack online.

“I was responsible for all electrons onboard the raft, and most of them behaved.”

18 December

First a shadow. Then an island.

After 43 days, Rapa Nui appeared on the horizon. The following day, the crew reached land.

“This is an honorable way of arriving on Easter Island. Epic.”
